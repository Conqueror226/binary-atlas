# Write a design brief for an industrial problem

Status: starter exercise; not completed portfolio work.

Frame one candidate project as an engineering design problem with stakeholders, testable requirements and a validation plan.

Scope: owned or explicitly authorized programs in an isolated lab. Commands describe the stated lab environment, not every host platform.

## Investigation steps

1. Pick one direction from the EngD alignment page, or your own. Describe the problem context without the solution: who has the problem, what it costs them today, and what they do instead. Mark every statement you have not confirmed with a real stakeholder as an assumption.

2. Write requirements that can each be tested, including the operational ones your administration experience suggests: runtime budget, skills needed to operate it, failure behavior, and data that may not leave the organization. List at least two alternative designs and why you would reject them.

3. Plan the validation: what you will measure, on which targets, against which baseline, and what result would make you abandon the design. Add scope, authorization and disclosure handling. Use the design brief template in the repository and keep it as a draft until someone else has challenged it.

## Evidence and validation

- A completed design brief with assumptions marked
- A requirements table where each row names its test
- A validation plan with a baseline and an abandonment criterion

## Learning output

A design brief you could send to a prospective supervisor or company, with its unverified parts visible.
